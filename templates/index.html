What is this?

Think of DTI as your "Financial Stress Level." It calculates how much of your monthly income must go to debt payments.

  • 36% (Healthy): Banks love this. You have plenty of wiggle room.
  • 36-43% (Caution): You can likely get a loan, but interest rates might be higher.
  • > 43% (Risky): Most banks will deny a mortgage here because you are "over-leveraged."

Where is your money going?

Distinguishing "Needs" (Fixed) vs. "Wants" (Variable)

Fixed vs. Variable Expenses

We categorized your spending into two buckets to help you plan:

Fixed Expenses (Needs)

These are Rent, Insurance, and Loans. Banks count these against your DTI. You usually can't change these easily.

Variable Expenses (Wants)

This is Dining, Shopping, and Entertainment. Banks usually ignore these for loans, but this is the easiest place to save money to build your surplus.

Quick Tip: If your DTI is too high, you must increase income or pay off a "Fixed" loan. Cutting coffee (Variable) helps savings, but does not improve your DTI score for a mortgage!
